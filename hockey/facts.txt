FAMOUS SCORING AND GOALTENDING RECORDS
Alex Ovechkin broke Wayne Gretzky's NHL regular-season goals record with career goal No. 895 on April 6, 2025, for the Washington Capitals. Do not give Gretzky as the modern answer to career regular-season goals leader. [R11]
Ovechkin's record-breaking 895th goal was a power-play goal against New York Islanders goalie Ilya Sorokin at UBS Arena; Tom Wilson supplied the pass. 895 is the milestone goal, not a claim about Ovechkin's current total. [R11]
Wayne Gretzky's NHL regular-season career totals: 894 goals, 1,963 assists and 2,857 points. His career points and assists totals are the famous NHL records; regular-season totals exclude playoffs. [R15]
Wayne Gretzky's single-season NHL records: 92 goals, 163 assists and 215 points. He scored 50 goals in 39 games during 1981-82. [R15]
Wayne Gretzky won nine Hart Trophies and ten Art Ross Trophies; his nine Harts included eight consecutive wins from 1980 through 1987. [R04,R15]
Wayne Gretzky won four Stanley Cups with Edmonton; his NHL teams were Edmonton Oilers, Los Angeles Kings, St. Louis Blues and New York Rangers. [R15]
Wayne Gretzky's No. 99 was retired league-wide at the 2000 NHL All-Star Game. [R15]
Martin Brodeur's NHL regular-season career records include 691 wins and 125 shutouts. His Stanley Cup titles with New Jersey came in 1995, 2000 and 2003. [R16]

NHL ORIGINS, ORIGINAL SIX AND EXPANSION
NHL stands for National Hockey League. It was organized in Montreal on November 26, 1917; the first NHL games were played December 19, 1917. [H01]
Original Six: Boston Bruins, Chicago Blackhawks, Detroit Red Wings, Montreal Canadiens, New York Rangers and Toronto Maple Leafs. [H01]
The Original Six era ran from 1942-43 through 1966-67; these were not the NHL's original six founding teams in 1917. [H01]
The 1967 expansion doubled the NHL from six to twelve teams. [H01,H02]
The six 1967 expansion teams: California Seals, Los Angeles Kings, Minnesota North Stars, Philadelphia Flyers, Pittsburgh Penguins and St. Louis Blues. [H01,H02]
First United States NHL franchise: Boston Bruins, beginning in 1924-25. Boston's first game was a 2-1 win over the Montreal Maroons on December 1, 1924. [H03]
Four WHA teams joined the NHL in 1979: Edmonton Oilers, Hartford Whalers, Quebec Nordiques and Winnipeg Jets. [H02]
First NHL regular-season outdoor game: Heritage Classic in Edmonton, November 22, 2003. First Winter Classic: Buffalo, January 1, 2008. [H01]

WHAT THE NHL TROPHIES MEAN
Hart Memorial Trophy: regular-season player judged most valuable to his team. [H04]
Art Ross Trophy: regular-season leader in points; points are goals plus assists. [H04]
Maurice 'Rocket' Richard Trophy: regular-season goals leader. [H04]
Vezina Trophy: best goaltender, selected by NHL general managers. [H04]
James Norris Memorial Trophy: defenseman with the best all-around ability at his position. [H04]
Calder Memorial Trophy: top NHL rookie. [H04]
Conn Smythe Trophy: MVP of the entire Stanley Cup Playoffs, not just the Final. [H04]
Frank J. Selke Trophy: forward who excels in defensive play. [H04]
Lady Byng Memorial Trophy: sportsmanship and gentlemanly conduct combined with high playing ability. [H04]
Jack Adams Award: coach judged to contribute most to his team's success. [H04]
Ted Lindsay Award: most outstanding player, selected by fellow NHLPA players; formerly the Lester B. Pearson Award. [H04]
Bill Masterton Memorial Trophy: perseverance, sportsmanship and dedication to hockey. [H04]
William M. Jennings Trophy: qualifying goalie or goalies for the team allowing the fewest regular-season goals. [H04]
King Clancy Memorial Trophy: leadership and humanitarian contributions. [H04]
Mark Messier NHL Leadership Award: leadership on and off the ice. [H04]

STANLEY CUP HISTORY AND DYNASTIES
The Stanley Cup is named for Lord Stanley of Preston, Sir Frederick Arthur Stanley, who donated it in 1892. [H05]
First Stanley Cup winner: Montreal Amateur Athletic Association (Montreal AAA), 1893. [H05,R12]
The Stanley Cup has been awarded exclusively to the NHL playoff champion beginning with 1926-27. [R12]
Most Stanley Cup titles by a team through 2026: Montreal Canadiens, 24, including the 1916 title before the NHL began. [R12]
Toronto's franchise has thirteen Stanley Cup titles, counting its Toronto Arenas (1918), Toronto St. Pats (1922) and Maple Leafs championships. [R12]
Detroit Red Wings have eleven Stanley Cup titles through 2026, the most by a United States-based team. [R12]
Montreal Canadiens won five consecutive Stanley Cups, 1956 through 1960. [H05,R12]
New York Islanders won four consecutive Stanley Cups, 1980 through 1983; Montreal won four straight from 1976 through 1979. [R12]
Edmonton Oilers won five Stanley Cups in seven years: 1984, 1985, 1987, 1988 and 1990. [R12]
Montreal Canadiens' most recent Stanley Cup through the completed 2026 playoffs was 1993; they are also the most recent Canadian team to win it at this snapshot. [R12]
Toronto Maple Leafs' most recent Stanley Cup through the completed 2026 playoffs was 1967; New York Rangers won in 1994. [R12]
The Cup was not awarded in 1919 because of the influenza epidemic or in 2005 during the canceled NHL season. [R12]
Winning players' names are engraved on the Stanley Cup; older bands are retired as space is needed. [H05]
The Stanley Cup is approximately 34.5 pounds and 35.25 inches tall. [H05]
Most Stanley Cups won as a player: Henri Richard, eleven, all with Montreal. [H06]

FAMOUS MOMENTS AND PHILADELPHIA TRIVIA
Bobby Orr's famous flying overtime goal won the 1970 Stanley Cup for Boston against St. Louis on May 10, 1970. [H10]
'Miracle on Ice': USA beat the Soviet Union 4-3 at the 1980 Lake Placid Olympics. Herb Brooks coached; Mike Eruzione scored the winning goal; Jim Craig was the USA goalie. [H11]
The 1980 USA-Soviet Olympic game was not the gold-medal-clinching game: USA subsequently beat Finland 4-2. [H11]
Paul Henderson scored Canada's deciding goal in Game 8 of the 1972 Summit Series against the Soviet Union: 34 seconds left, 6-5 final, Moscow, September 28, 1972. [H12]
Willie O'Ree became the NHL's first Black player on January 18, 1958, for Boston against Montreal. [H03]
Philadelphia Flyers won back-to-back Stanley Cups in 1974 and 1975 under coach Fred Shero. [H13,R12]
Philadelphia's 1970s teams were the 'Broad Street Bullies'. [H13]
Bernie Parent was Philadelphia's goalie for both 1974 and 1975 Cup wins and won the Conn Smythe and Vezina trophies in both seasons. [H13]
Bob Kelly scored Philadelphia's Cup-winning goal against Buffalo in Game 6 of the 1975 Final. [H13]

RULES AND COMMON HOCKEY TERMS
NHL regulation: three periods of twenty minutes each, totaling sixty minutes. [H14]
Standard NHL rink: 200 feet long and 85 feet wide. [H14]
NHL regular-season overtime normally uses three skaters and a goalie per side, lasts up to five minutes, and is sudden death. [H14]
Offside: an attacking player enters the attacking zone ahead of the puck, subject to the rulebook's exceptions and definitions. [H14]
Hat trick: one player scores three goals in a game. Natural hat trick: one player scores three consecutive goals in a game. The term 'hat trick' originated in cricket. [H15,H16]
A player's points equal goals plus assists. A goal can be credited with no more than two assists. [H15]
Shorthanded goal: scored while the scoring team has fewer players because of penalties. [H15]
Save percentage: saves divided by shots faced. Shooting percentage: goals divided by shots taken. [H15]
Stanley Cup Playoffs: sixteen teams; every series is best-of-seven, requiring four wins to advance. [H14]
